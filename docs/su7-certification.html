Phases and exclusion of the printed rounding intervals

For signed coefficients m, parity signs s ∈ {+1,−1}, and positive integer charges c, define

F(α) = ∑(m,s,c) m ∑n≥1 sn cos(cπnα)/n5.

Each printed phase is expanded to its rounding interval of half-width 0.0005. Each computed minimum is isolated in an interval of half-width 10−6. The nine decimal places below are numerical approximations, not nine certified decimal places.

Reconstruction from representations

Q = 2T44 = E57 + E75 commutes with P6 and B = P5P′5. Their common eigenbasis has weights (q,P6,B): three copies of (0,+,+), one (0,−,−), one (0,−,+), and (±1,−,−). Direct enumeration of Sym², Sym³ and 7⊗7̄−1 gives dimensions 28, 84 and 48, respectively; Newton identities independently check both symmetric powers.

In Sym³, the antiperiodic c = 1 coefficient is 12. It includes the lower charges of the quartet, whose charges are ±3 and ±1. Removing that contribution is not justified by the representation. Reversing the product of intrinsic parities sends s to −s and reproduces the other four tables. The representation content belongs to Komori–Maru; the earlier SU(7) reconstruction is documented in Marín’s Part VI. [KM25] [Marín VI]

The last two terms in the 84 formula have subscripts 48 in PDF equation (76), HTML equation (82). This appears to be an index typo. The reconstruction assigns each field the parities of its own representation; the typo has not been established as the cause of the numerical discrepancy.

The gauge question and its assumptions

On a flat background FMN = 0, with background gauge fixing and compatible Faddeev–Popov ghost parities, Aμ contributes four components, A5 one, and the complex ghost subtracts two in the positive P6 sector: 4 + 1 − 2 = 3. A6 contributes one in the other sector. For charged pairs, reversing both fifth-direction parities gives an equivalent spectrum because Q exchanges the reflection eigenspaces.

Under these assumptions the candidate gauge triples (m,s,c) are (−3/4,+,2), (−3/2,+,1), (−3,−,1). The printed triples are (−1,+,2), (−2,+,1), (−7/2,−,1). The 3+1 candidate predates this certification and is recorded in Part VII. It also fails to reproduce the printed phases. Choosing the physical determinant still requires a complete gauge and boundary analysis. The familiar five-dimensional gauge–ghost factor −3 in Haba–Yamashita does not by itself settle that six-dimensional question. [Marín VII] [HY04]

Why the minimum certificate is global

Periodicity F(α+2) = F(α) and reflection F(−α) = F(α) reduce the domain to [0,1]. Opposite signs of F′ isolate a root in a strictly convex neighborhood. Outside that neighborhood, an exhaustive interval partition proves that F exceeds its value at a reference point inside the neighborhood. The partition has no gaps. Rational endpoints and positive margins are included in the certificate JSON.

|Rj,N| ≤ ∑ |m|(πc)j / [(4−j)N4−j],   j = 0,1,2.

This standard integral bound controls the Fourier tail. The bound ∑ |m|(πc)³ζ(2) controls the variation of F″. Native arithmetic encloses π with Machin’s formula and rational alternating series, reduces phases exactly, and uses Taylor expansions with explicit remainders for sine and cosine. Integer division rounds outward. Sage/Arb independently evaluates each interval without reusing that trigonometric implementation.

For k = R5/R6 ≥ 10, restored sixth-direction terms are bounded using

g(x) = coth(x) + x csch²(x) + (2/3)x² coth(x)csch²(x).

The remainder g−1 decays exponentially. The rational bound uses π > 3 and e6 > 400; its contribution to F′ is too small to reconcile any printed phase interval. This result assumes the stated massless determinant and matter contents. Part VI already discussed finite-k suppression; this dossier supplies an explicit uniform bound.

Formula-by-formula comparison and attribution

The comparison matches mathematical expressions after making normalization and variable changes explicit. An antecedent for an identity does not imply that two physical models are equivalent. The references identify checked sources for the formulas and their applications.

1. Periodic and antiperiodic expansions

Write S±(x) = ∑n≥1(±1)ncos(nx)/n⁵. Haba, Takenaga and Toshifumi Yamashita give the following expansions in equations (10–11), including higher terms. For x > 0, their ln(x²) equals 2 ln x. [HTY05]

S+(x) = ζ(5) − ζ(3)x²/2 + x⁴[25/12 − ln x]/24 + O(x⁶),
S−(x) = −15ζ(5)/16 + 3ζ(3)x²/8 − x⁴ ln 2/24 + O(x⁶).

The even extension uses ln|x|. These kernels and their logarithmic term are established formulas.

2. The three-coefficient potential has a direct antecedent

With x = πα > 0 and the constant term removed, define Aj = ∑s=+1 mcj, Bj = ∑s=−1 mcj, and

D = A2 − 3B2/4,
G = (25/12)A4 − ∑s=+1 mc⁴ ln c − B4 ln 2,
f(x) = −ζ(3)Dx²/2 + x⁴[G − A4 ln x]/24.

For the finite charge list, F(x/π)−F(0) = f(x)+O(x⁶) as x→0. Sakamoto and Takenaga’s equation (15) has exactly this functional form, with the coefficient map

D = C(2),   A4 = C(3),   G = (25/12)C(3) + C(4) ln 2.

Their equations (16–18) specify discrete coefficients from matter multiplicities in an SU(3) model. The SU(7) representation dictionary and allowed coefficient values differ. This is a match of analytic structure, not an identification of the models. Credit for this antecedent belongs to Makoto Sakamoto and Kazunori Takenaga, in addition to the earlier kernel expansions. [ST07]

3. Stationary curvature and the fixed-mass reduction

Differentiating the displayed f and imposing f′(x*) = 0 at x* > 0 gives the algebraic consequences

f″(x*) = 2ζ(3)D − A4x*²/6,
μ = f″(x*)/x*²,
x*² = 12ζ(3)D/(6μ + A4),
ln x* = (G − 3μ)/A4 − 3/4.

The last two expressions require nonzero denominators. They are exact for f; the full Fourier potential has higher-order corrections. Part VII develops their SU(7) fixed-mass application. The general identities are algebraic consequences of the established potential. Sakamoto–Takenaga’s equation (37) already eliminates the logarithm in the special case C(2) = 0; it is not the general formula printed above. [Marín VII] [ST07]

Set K = √3 mWg4/(2π³). Since F″(α) = π²f″(x) within the truncation, μ = mh²/(Kπ²)². The full-potential diagnostic mhα/√F″ = K follows directly from the mass formula; Part VI uses it as a consistency check, not an independent mass law. [KM25] [Marín VI]

4. Lambert W and its branches

For D > 0 and A4 > 0, set y = x², d = 12ζ(3)D/A4 and b = (4G−A4)/(2A4). The stationarity equation becomes

y(b−ln y) = d,   y = −d/Wj(−d e−b),
μ = −A4[Wj(−d e−b)+1]/6.

W(z)eW(z) = z defines the classical Lambert function; Corless, Gonnet, Hare, Jeffrey and Knuth provide the standard modern reference for its branches. When b−1−ln d > 0, W−1 yields a local minimum and W0 a local maximum of f. At equality the stationary points merge and the curvature vanishes. These branch assignments use the stated signs, and neither root is guaranteed to lie in the small-angle regime. Part VII applies this standard inversion. A full-Fourier global minimum requires a separate proof. [Corless96] [Marín VII]

5. Duplication, endpoint factors and the finite function basis

The elementary separation into even and odd n gives the exact relation

S−(x) = S+(2x)/16 − S+(x).

Cacciapaglia, Cornell, Deandrea, Isnard, Pasechnik, Preda and Wang explicitly use this identity and its endpoint values in equations (2.11–2.13) of their PDF, (11–13) in HTML. Their functions are ℱ±(a) = (3/2)S±(2πa). This is a relevant published antecedent, not a claim that they originated the mathematical identity. [CCD24]

For the lab’s charges c = 1,2,3, substitution reduces the six signed kernels to five periodic frequencies 1,2,3,4,6. The endpoint factor 31/16 is 1+15/16. In Part VII notation,

F(1)−F(0) = (31/16)ζ(5)Wend,   Wend = ∑c odd (−s)m.

These are direct deductions from the established kernel identity. The SU(7) integer classification and nonnegative generator relations in Part VII are more specific statements and retain that attribution. Wend is the quantity called W in the seed-shift theorem; it is distinct from the Lambert function.

6. SU(7) congruences and their scope

8D − 2A4 ≡ 3 (mod 6).

Part VII obtains this model-specific law from the eight allowed matter generators and either stated gauge contribution, using the elementary identity c⁴ ≡ c² (mod 3). Every matter generator shifts 8D−2A4 by a multiple of six, while the gauge contribution has residue three. The congruence holds for both gauge choices; the parity of 8D differs. The Lean proof certifies this application. [Marín VII]

Likewise, D = (S+7Δ)/8 with S = A2+B2 and Δ = A2−B2 is a rearrangement of the periodic/antiperiodic curvature weights. A trace formulation must also specify whether opposite charges are counted separately. Higher derivatives at the origin cannot be obtained by treating ζ(1) as finite: the quartic term contains the logarithm displayed above.

What remains open

  • Physical gauge determinant: Complete written gauge-fixed boundary problem and literature/author comparison; algebraic 3+1 counting alone does not settle every physical assumption.
  • Other model contents and multiple Wilson phases: An individual continuum certificate; ten benchmarks do not certify arbitrary scans.
  • Moment upper bounds: Keep small-angle hypotheses. A certified full-Fourier benchmark does not promote the moment ceiling into a full-potential theorem.
  • Neutrinos and collider comparisons: Algebraic identities may be formalized; numerical diagonalization, experimental inputs and phenomenological viability require their own checks.
  • Thermal bounce and cosmological history: Validated differential-equation solutions and full error propagation before claiming interval-certified transition predictions.
  • Real analysis in Lean: Formalize convergence, derivative interchange, tail estimates, convexity and the certificate checker. Present analytic proofs are computer-assisted, not Lean-certified.

Comparison with public implementations

The comparison is based on inspected source and documentation, not on running those repositories. Our existing HiggsTools, warped-running, anomaly and thermal integrations are retained; these are not proposed as missing features.

Yoon's inspected solve.jl adjusts a parameter to a target Higgs mass; agreement after that fit is not an independent mass prediction. It uses numerical quadrature and minimum tolerances. Its notebooks distinguish running quantities and pole inputs. The appropriate cross-check is a shared SO(5) benchmark with matching conventions, not a direct comparison of its numbers with our SU(7) table. The lattice repository supplies a useful validation pattern, but its scalar field is not automatically a Wilson-line Higgs.

Priority improvements: (1) explicit mass and coupling matching with theory uncertainties, (2) reproduction of a published CMS likelihood point followed by a GHU signal implementation, (3) one externally reproduced SO(5) reference point. Reusing foreign source code additionally requires checking its license; no foreign code is copied into the laboratory in this update.

  • CMS Combine: Preferred statistical engine for the released CMS dijet workspace. Need GHU signal templates and nuisance treatment; existing cards implement other models.
  • pyhf: HistFactory JSON likelihoods. It is not a direct reader of arbitrary Combine ROOT workspaces.
  • Lilith-2: Higgs signal-strength likelihoods from couplings or rates. README names a 2019 database and a 2022 beta; these must not be described as all current LHC data.
  • HiggsTools: Already integrated in this laboratory through matching input/result records. Hosted on GitLab. Extend model-specific predictions before treating a generic scalar scenario as a GHU fit.
  • CMS H to four-lepton outreach analysis: ROOT RDataFrame reconstruction on reduced CMS Open Data. Useful for an educational event display or histogram; not a replacement for the precision mass measurement.
  • CMS candidate event dataset: Education/outreach subset; the source explicitly says it is unsuitable for a full physics analysis.
  • ATLAS outreach framework: Reproducible examples of data and simulation distributions; educational input must retain its scope.

The search is targeted, not exhaustive. A missing repository in these results establishes neither absence nor scientific novelty.

All bounds are rounded outward. The numerical entries bound the difference from the exact N = 2048 finite-sum curvature mass at the certified root interval's midpoint. They are conservative upper bounds, not measured errors or estimates of their typical size. The full mass enclosure already includes root location, the Fourier tail and Arb rounding. Its half-width must not be added a second time.

Writing a₀ for the midpoint, h for the root radius, a₋ for the lower phase endpoint, K = √3 mW g₄/(2π³), and L₃ = ζ(2) Σ |M| (πq)³, the tail in F″ is at most E₂ = Σ |M| (πq)²/(2N²). For positive curvature bounds m ≤ F″ ≤ M valid along the comparison, the mass contributions are bounded by K E₂/(2a₀√m) and K h[L₃/(2a₋√m) + √M/a₋²]. The script widens the curvature interval to cover the finite/infinite-sum interpolation before applying these inequalities.

The registered PDG 2025 W-mass input is 80.3692 ± 0.0133 GeV. At fixed potential geometry, mₕ ∝ g₄mW and 1/R₅ ∝ mW. Exact rational rescaling keeps the central update from 80.4 GeV separate from the propagated input uncertainty. A displayed g₄ ±10% range is a chosen scenario, with no assigned probability. Printed and 3+1 gauge prescriptions remain separate hypotheses.

The physical theory uncertainty is not quantified. Curvature-to-pole matching, higher loops, perturbative control and the physical model completion remain open. Their missing contributions are stored as null, never as zero. No total error bar, combined likelihood or exclusion significance follows from this budget.
