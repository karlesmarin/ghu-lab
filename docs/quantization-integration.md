## Experimental evidence and the new theorems

Measured data can support a physical realization of a theorem's assumptions and
test its observable consequences. They do not change the logical validity of a
proved conditional theorem. Agreement must be assessed against an explicit
prediction, uncertainties and alternatives; an allowed parameter point is not
a detection of the model.

The current quantization tool supplies no map from its inputs to particle masses,
couplings, production rates or a likelihood. Its `fullModelConsistency` remains
`not_checked`. Consequently no ATLAS/CMS agreement, fit probability or experimental
validation of these quantization examples is assigned.

Both resonance publications report no significant new-particle signal in their
respective searches. Matching their *theory benchmark curves* checks the numerical
implementation, not agreement of a new model with measured data. The KK-gluon
module retains its documented ATLAS high-mass deviation and PDF dependence.
ATLAS and CMS are experiments at CERN; CERN is not an additional independent
measurement to count alongside those same datasets.

To establish a physical bridge, specify a single complete candidate (global group,
representations, action and admissible backgrounds), match its quantized
coefficients to the parameters of an observable calculation, and propagate
theoretical and experimental uncertainties with their correlations. Then report
the dataset/version, prediction, comparison statistic and scope together.
Independent predictions or held-out measurements are needed to distinguish a
successful prediction from tuning to the same inputs. This bridge remains open;
the existing collider data are preserved and accessible, without relabeling them
as evidence for the new arithmetic examples.
