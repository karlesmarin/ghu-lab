what
: The SU(N) builder gives the potential and the vacuum; the boundary-conditions section says which
  boundary conditions are the same theory. Neither says what the theory **has**. This does: the
  four-dimensional fields of any 5D SU(N) model on S¹/Z₂, massless and towered, with their quantum
  numbers under the unbroken group. It is Table 1 of every paper in this corner of the field.

  One rule does all of it — Haba–Yamashita eq. (3.5): the mode expansion is fixed by the pair of Z₂
  parities a state carries, and only **(+,+)** has a zero mode. Two riders carry the physics.
  **A_y has the opposite parity to A_μ**, so the massless vectors are the unbroken group and the
  massless scalars are exactly where the vectors are not — which is where the Higgs candidates
  live. And **a Dirac fermion's two chiralities carry opposite parities**, because the orbifold
  condition carries a γ₅: at most one of them can be (+,+), so a massless 4D fermion here is
  automatically **chiral**. That is the whole reason for orbifolding, and the panel says whether
  this particular boundary condition delivers it.

  The section takes no model of its own: it shares the builder's, so changing the boundary
  condition or the bulk in either panel moves both.

why
: Because "what does this theory contain" is the first question of model building and the
  instrument could not answer it. And because the answer is not where a reader expects: the
  massless content follows from the **parities**, not from evaluating a Kaluza-Klein family at
  n = 0 — a (+,+) state expands in cos and has a zero mode, a (−,−) state expands in sin and has
  none. A tower drawn the second way puts states at zero mass that are not there, so this panel
  publishes the **families** — a multiplicity, an offset and a charge, which is what the sources
  print — and computes the massless content separately. What it does not do, it says.

so
: Two things fell out that were not the point and are worth more than it.

  **The spectrum and the potential are the same data.** A cosine in the potential is a pair of
  states in the spectrum: `m·cos(nπ(c·θ − d))` is two states at charge ±c/2, with d = 1 the
  half-integer tower. So summing cos(2πnQ) over the states must reproduce the potential's own
  bracket — the potential counts each ± pair once and the states count both, so the factor is
  exactly 2. Asserted, not assumed, and checked on 96 cases in the harness at 5·10⁻¹⁵ and again on
  every render of the page. Two constructions that share no code, one object.

  **And the deepest point is not always a vacuum.** On their own §4.3 SU(6) with four
  fundamentals, the minimum sits at θ = 1 — an **end** of the fundamental domain, which is the
  other symmetric point and not a broken vacuum. The panel now says so, and names the boundary
  condition it belongs to: the gauge-equivalent one a single class move away. Three sections of
  this instrument meet at that sentence.
