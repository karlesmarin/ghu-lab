what
: Part VII maximises `1/R₅` over the lattice of bulk contents and publishes a **ceiling**. Part
  VIII runs the same map **backwards**: given a target box in the two observables the closed form
  predicts, it returns a bulk content or a **named certificate** that none exists. The instrument
  had the ceiling and none of the inverse. It has both now, in two sections that sit next to
  Hierarchy because they are the same map read the other way.

  **Design a scale** does the deciding. Type a scale — or click the axis — and the page walks the
  rungs, issuing one of five certificates where nothing sits: `floor` (identity (II) asking for an
  A₄ below the *empty* content, which non-negative multiplicities forbid), `cone`, `congruence`,
  `dual` (an exact rational Farkas pair) and `exhaustion` (the rung is finite and was enumerated).
  Two more things are reported and are explicitly **not** certificates: `rung`, which excludes the
  rung and not the target, and `budget`, which means the enumeration was stopped. A design is only
  a design once the **exact potential** agrees that the electroweak point is the deepest place on
  (0, 1] — `W > 0` compares the two symmetric points and nothing else.

  **Count a rung** does the counting. A dynamic programme over the two partial moments gives
  `N(A₄, 8D)` for every A₄ at once, in about twenty milliseconds, where the enumerator that built
  the same contents one by one took twenty-five minutes and ran out of budget below 5 TeV. The
  four rung totals — 69 022 464 contents — land exactly on what that enumerator built, by an
  algorithm that shares nothing with it. The section also proves and checks the recurrence
  `N(A₄, 8D+6) = N(A₄, 8D) − P`, which is why the four counting curves superpose, and reads the
  fibre of the measured-mass point with Part VII's completeness theorem: 81 contents there share
  all five coordinates, so they are not 81 models that agree — they are 81 ways to build one
  potential, one of 19 multiplets and one of 198.

why
: A ceiling is a bound; the inverse direction reports a **set**, and the set is not what the
  bound suggests. The clusters the instrument now draws are **finite point sets** — rung one is
  35 values of `1/R₅`, about 31.5 GeV apart — and between the rung-three and rung-one clusters
  there are **2682 GeV** with nothing in them, forty-five times the widest gap *inside* either
  cluster. A finite set is disconnected for free; a macroscopic stretch closed by an exact
  rational certificate is not.

  Nothing in the record moves: this is Part VIII arriving in the instrument, not a correction to
  anything already deposited.

so
: Nothing to redo, and three defects paid for on the way — all of them in this page's own code,
  all now guarded. The first drawing
  painted each cluster as a **bar**, which is the misreading the paper warns against; it is a comb
  now, computed here. The point sweep then hit a **budget cap** on rung three and printed 54
  points as though they were 65, with no mention of the cut — the cap is derived from the same
  threshold that chose the rung, and a deliberately capped sweep is now part of the harness. And
  the fibre panel picked its class by **size**, which on this rung gives 86 and not the paper's
  81: two classes there have a single 2W, and the one the paper means is the one at the measured
  Higgs mass. Each class is one potential, so it has one scale, and that is what selects it.
